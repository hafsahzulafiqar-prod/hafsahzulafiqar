Overview

  1. Problem

    Coaches were complaining. Kahunas's go-to-market platform, the software they run their day-to-day coaching business on, had grown feature by feature without a structured UX pass, and coaches found it hard to navigate. So I started by finding out exactly where and why they got lost.

  2. Central Question

    How might we make the platform easier to navigate for coaches running their business on it every day, while also surfacing more upselling opportunities, without the redesign ever feeling like a sales push?

  3. Result

    The audit led to a rebuilt navigation, with upselling moved into the flow instead of bolted on. In parallel, I revamped the AI module so coaches could get answers straight from the docs instead of filing a ticket. User response to the platform went up and support ticket volume went down.

The full story

I joined Kahunas as Product Designer in October 2024, with the GTM app as my first target: a product coaches relied on daily, but one that hadn't had a structured UX pass in some time. Rather than redesigning from a hunch, I ran a full UX audit first, mapping every flow a coach actually uses, from onboarding a new client to tracking their progress to billing, to find where coaches were dropping off or working around the product instead of with it.

Research

I didn't want to redesign from a hunch, so I ran a full UX audit of the GTM app first. I mapped every flow a coach actually uses, from onboarding a new client to tracking their progress to billing. The audit showed me where coaches were dropping off, where they were working around the product instead of with it, and where the interface asked them to think like the software instead of the other way around. That became the brief for the redesign.

UX audit findings board

Project challenges

01. Navigation had grown organically, feature by feature, with no structured pass to pull it back together, so coaches were spending real effort just finding their way around software they used every day.
02. Simplifying navigation and growing revenue pulled in opposite directions on the surface: making the platform easier to use could easily mean stripping out the very moments where an upgrade or add-on got surfaced. The brief was to do both at once, not trade one for the other.

Design considerations

The audit gave me one test for every decision: does this make the platform easier to navigate and surface a timely upsell, or does it only do one of the two?

Key points: rebuild the core navigation around how coaches actually move through their day, not how the product happened to grow; surface upgrade and add-on moments contextually, inside the flow a coach is already in, instead of a generic sales banner; and replace "file a ticket" with "ask the product" wherever a coach just needed an answer, not a human.

Core features

Three workstreams, one thread: make the software match how coaches actually work, and look for the moments that could carry an upsell without getting in the way.

A navigation structure coaches actually understand

The GTM app's information architecture was redrawn around a coach's real day-to-day, client management, programs, billing, rather than the structure the product happened to grow into.

How it works

Flows that used to cross several disconnected sections were consolidated around the task a coach was actually trying to complete.

Upselling built into the flow, not bolted on

Upgrade and add-on prompts surface at the specific moment a coach is already considering that decision, instead of a generic banner competing for attention everywhere else.

Video placeholder
How it works

Contextual prompts are tied to real usage signals, so the suggestion matches what the coach is already trying to do.

An AI module for reading the docs, so coaches don't have to

The existing AI-driven module was revamped so a coach can ask a direct question and get an answer sourced from the product's own documentation, instead of opening a support ticket and waiting.

Video placeholder
How it works

The module's interaction model was simplified and made clearer about what it was doing and why at each step, which is what brought ticket volume down, not just the AI answer itself.

The complaint was that the platform was hard to navigate, so I rebuilt navigation around how coaches actually work, moved upselling into the flow instead of bolting it on, and gave coaches an AI module for getting answers from the docs instead of a support ticket. That turned Kahunas's most common complaint into one of its clearest wins:
