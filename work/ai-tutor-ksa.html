Mualim: designing an AI tutor for a new generation of learners.

A product design consulting engagement for Mualim, an AI ed-tech product built for the Saudi Arabian market — an AI-driven tutor for kids, designed to make AI-driven learning approachable from the first session.

Product Design Consultant Consulting AI · Ed-Tech · KSA

The brief

Mualim set out to build an AI tutor for kids — a product meant to make AI-driven learning feel natural and engaging for a young audience in the Saudi Arabian market, rather than a novelty bolted onto a traditional ed-tech app. I was brought in as product design consultant, owning the design work end to end: research, strategy, prototyping, and shipping the final designs.

Research first

Designing for kids means designing for two audiences at once — the child actually using the product, and the parent or guardian deciding whether to trust it with their kid's learning. My UX research focused on understanding how each side needed to experience the product differently: what makes an AI tutor feel safe, engaging, and genuinely useful to a child, and what makes it feel trustworthy and transparent to the adult overseeing it. That split informed every decision that followed.

Designing for a child's attention and a parent's trust, at the same time — in a single interface.

Strategy

With the research in hand, I shaped the product strategy around how an AI tutor should actually behave with a young learner: how it introduces itself, how it paces a lesson, how it responds when a child gets something wrong, and how it surfaces progress back to a parent without making the experience feel like it's being graded. My goal was a strategy that treated "AI-driven learning" as a promise to make good on through interaction design, not just a label on the product.

Prototyping and shipping

From there, I built out the prototype — testing the tutor's core interaction flows, iterating on how lessons were structured and how the interface communicated with a child in an age-appropriate way — before taking the designs through to shipped, production-ready screens. As a consultant, that meant handing off work that a product and engineering team could build directly from, with the reasoning behind each decision documented alongside it.

The throughline

Mualim sits furthest from my other case studies in category — ed-tech for kids, rather than DTC, B2B SaaS, or healthcare — but the process is the same one that shows up everywhere else in my work: understand the real people on both sides of the product before designing anything, then let that understanding drive the strategy, the prototype, and what actually ships.
